Assignment 2: Food Delivery Platform Database Design
Database Systems 1

Objective:

In this assignment, you will design a database from scratch for a food delivery platform. You have full creative control over the design, but you must be able to justify every decision you make. AI is allowed and encouraged as a tool, but your reasoning, assumptions, and design choices must be your own. 

The Platform

You are hired as the database designer for a new food delivery startup. The platform connects customers, restaurants, and delivery drivers. Here is what you know:

Customers can browse restaurants, place orders with multiple items, and leave reviews.
Restaurants manage their own menus, which change over time. Items get added, removed, and repriced.
Drivers pick up orders and deliver them. Their location is tracked during delivery.
The platform runs promotions including discount codes that apply to certain restaurants, certain menu categories, or the entire platform.
The business needs to track everything: who ordered what, when, which driver delivered it, how long it took, and whether the customer was satisfied.
The description above is intentionally incomplete. Part of your job is to fill in the gaps.

Part 1: Assumptions

Before designing anything, list every assumption you made to fill in missing details from the description. What did the requirements not tell you that you had to decide yourself? Minimum 8 assumptions. Be specific.

Part 2: ER Diagram

Design a full ER diagram for the platform. Your diagram must include:

All entities with their attributes
All relationships between entities
Cardinality and participation constraints for every relationship
Draw your ER diagram using traditional ER notation as covered in class. You may use ExcalidrawLinks to an external site. or draw it by hand. If hand-drawn, take a clear photo and include it in your submission.

Part 3: Decision Log

Pick 5 specific design decisions where you had a real choice between two options. For each one, answer:

What were the two options you considered?
What did you choose?
Why did you choose it over the alternative?
This is the most important part of the assignment. AI can generate a diagram, but this section must reflect your own thinking about your own design.

Part 4: Relational Tables

Convert your ER diagram into database tables. For each table, clearly show:

All column names and data types
Primary keys
Foreign keys and what they reference
Part 5: Normalization Check

Go through your tables and verify they satisfy 1NF, 2NF, and 3NF. If any table violates a normal form, fix it and explain what you changed and why.

Part 6: Open Question

The startup is growing and wants to add a subscription model. Premium customers pay a monthly fee and get free delivery and priority support. How would you change your database design to support this? You do not need to redraw the full diagram. Explain in writing what changes, what new tables or columns you would add, and why.

What to Submit:
A Word document or PDF containing all six parts. Include your ER diagram as an image in Part 2.

Grading:

Assumptions: 10 pts
ER Diagram: 25 pts
Completeness of entities and attributes
Correct cardinality and participation constraints
Logical and consistent design
Decision Log: 25 pts
Quality of reasoning for each decision
Evidence of genuine tradeoff analysis
Database Tables: 20 pts
Correct conversion from ER diagram
Correct primary and foreign keys
Normalization Check: 10 pts
Open Question: 10 pts
AI Policy:
AI is allowed and encouraged as a tool. However, your Decision Log and Open Question must reflect your own thinking and your own design choices.
